# Evaluating synthetic tabular data: fidelity, utility, privacy and valid inference

Companion code for the Medium article of the same name, the sequel to
[*Advanced Techniques for Generating Synthetic Data*](https://towardsdev.com/advanced-techniques-for-generating-synthetic-data-bf040ff8de66) (2024).

The 2024 article was about producing synthetic data. This one is about judging it. Six generators are fitted to a third of the UCI Adult census extract and each synthetic table is scored on three axes (fidelity, predictive utility, privacy). A second study asks whether confidence intervals computed from synthetic data cover the true parameter, first under a known data-generating mechanism and then with the whole Adult file treated as the population, and compares the combining rules of Raghunathan, Reiter and Rubin (2003) and Raab, Nowok and Dibben (2016) with analysing the synthetic data as if it were real.

## Run

From this folder:

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python run_all.py
```

Python 3.13. The first run downloads the PMLB copy of Adult (about 0.5 MB, checked against its SHA-256) into `data/`. The pipeline takes about twenty minutes on two cores and writes:

- `results/summary.txt`: every number quoted in the article
- `results/axes.json`: fidelity, utility and privacy of each synthetic table
- `results/coverage_mechanism.json`: coverage under the known mechanism (2,000 replications)
- `results/coverage_adult.json`: coverage with Adult as the population (1,000 replications)
- `figures/fig1_coefficients.png` to `figures/fig5_coverage_adult.png`

All randomness is seeded, and nearest-neighbour searches and normal draws are computed in a way that does not depend on the BLAS or LAPACK build, so a rerun on another machine gives the same numbers up to floating-point rounding. The workflow in `.github/workflows/synthetic-data-evaluation.yml` reruns the pipeline on every change to this folder and compares the new summary with the committed one, number by number (each number may move by 0.006, by one unit in its last printed digit, or by 1% if it is 1 or larger, because floating-point kernels differ slightly between machines).

The CTGAN and TVAE tables are not regenerated by `run_all.py`. They were produced by `deep.py` (300 epochs each, CPU; training times in `results/deep_training.txt`) and are committed in `data/synthetic/`; rerunning that script needs `requirements-deep.txt` and will not reproduce the tables bit for bit.

## Design

**Data.** Adult has 48,842 records and 15 columns (6 numeric, 9 categorical including income). It is split into three random thirds: *train* (the confidential data; every generator sees only this), *holdout* (test set and privacy non-members) and *reference* (an independent real sample that shows what an ideal generator would score).

**Generators** (`generators.py`, `deep.py`).

- independent marginals: each column resampled on its own;
- Gaussian copula with empirical marginals (randomised probability integral transform for numeric columns, frequency intervals for categorical ones);
- sequential CART in the synthpop style: each column is drawn from the leaf of a tree grown on the columns before it, with income last;
- SMOTE as a whole-table generator, within income classes;
- CTGAN and TVAE from the `ctgan` package;
- two references: the training data itself and the independent real sample.

**Part 1: three axes** (`evaluate_axes.py`, `metrics.py`).

- Fidelity: Kolmogorov–Smirnov and total variation distances per column, total variation distance of every two-way table, a cross-fitted gradient-boosting discriminator (AUC and pMSE), with and without folds that keep each synthetic record together with its nearest real record, and logical rules that hold in the real data.
- Utility: train on synthetic, test on real (gradient boosting for income > $50K), and the coefficients of a logistic regression compared with the real estimate.
- Privacy: exact copies, distance to the closest training record, the share of synthetic records closer to train than to the equally large holdout, and a distance-based membership-inference attack (AUC and true-positive rate at 1% false positives).

**Part 2: inference** (`inference.py`, `theory.py`, `mechanism.py`, `coverage_mechanism.py`, `coverage_adult.py`).

- Simple synthesis draws all m data sets from one fitted generator; proper synthesis refits the generator on a bootstrap resample before each draw.
- Rules for the variance of the average estimate: as if real (ū/m), simple (ū(k + 1/m)), proper (ū(k + (1 + k)/m)), and Raghunathan–Reiter–Rubin ((1 + 1/m)b − ū, with Reiter's 2002 adjustment when negative).
- `theory.py` gives closed-form coverage for a correctly specified generator; `coverage_mechanism.py` checks it by simulation and adds the generic generators.
- `coverage_adult.py` treats the 48,842 records as the population, draws samples of 2,000, and compares every rule's intervals with the population coefficients.

## Data

Adult is the 1994 US Census extract prepared by Barry Becker and Ronny Kohavi (UCI Machine Learning Repository, doi:10.24432/C5XW20), here in the PMLB copy, which stores categories as alphabetical integer codes and codes income as `<=50K` = 1. `data.py` restores the original labels.
