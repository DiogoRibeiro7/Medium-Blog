# BEA/FRED nonresidential fixed investment (1964–2018)

Primary source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *Shares of gross domestic product: Gross private domestic investment: Fixed investment: Nonresidential* (BEA NIPA series), accessed through Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED series `A008RE1A156NBEA`: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/A008RE1A156NBEA . The table was consulted 2026-11-02. Annual, not seasonally adjusted, percent of nominal GDP; values reproduced at FRED's published one-decimal precision. The latest available source vintage can be revised by BEA; this CSV freezes its then-current 1964–2018 window.

Comparison series: BEA/FRED gross private domestic investment / GDP (already frozen in `real_growth_private_investment_1964_2018.csv`; see `macro_sources.md`). The gross aggregate includes residential investment and changes in private inventories. **Nonresidential fixed investment excludes both** but includes commercial structures, equipment, and intellectual-property products; it is not confined to corporations or manufacturing. Subtracting the series from gross private domestic investment yields the sum of residential fixed investment and private inventory change, which can be negative; therefore the difference is not a pure measure of housing investment.

Percentage-of-GDP movements can be caused by changes in investment, GDP, relative prices, or the business cycle. This paired time series is descriptive and does not estimate an effect of the Kennedy, Reagan, Bush, or 2017 tax reforms. In particular, 2018 follows one year after the 2017 tax act and cannot establish a long-run effect. The manuscript treats firm-level empirical work on the law separately from this chronology.
