# Historical top marginal individual income-tax rate transcription

The CSV records 55 consecutive annual values (1964–2018) of the **highest
bracket rate for U.S. individual regular income tax**. Source: U.S. Internal
Revenue Service (IRS), Statistics of Income Historical Table 23, as distributed
without interpolation by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis:

- [FRED IITTRHB annual table](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/IITTRHB)
- [IRS Historical Table 23](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-historical-table-23)

The FRED release identifies the original producer as the IRS, original units
as percent, annual frequency, and a historical endpoint of 2018. The series
was last updated in FRED on 14 January 2021. Values here were manually
transcribed from the displayed FRED table and cross-checked against the
regime changes described in Congressional Research Service reports.

This is a **statutory marginal rate**, not a realized effective tax rate,
not a rate paid on total income, not the maximum rate on all kinds of income,
and not the highest marginal rate on earned labor income after interacting
with payroll and other taxes. Bracket thresholds, taxable-base rules,
deductions, surtaxes, capital-gains treatment, and enforcement all changed.
The 1970s 70% top statutory rate applied to some non-earned income, while
the rate on earned income was capped at 50%, so treating it as a universal
top wage rate would be misleading.

The 1964–2018 window captures the Kennedy–Johnson, Reagan, Bush,
Clinton, and original 2017 TCJA statutory changes. It intentionally stops
at 2018 because the cited FRED/IRS table ends there. Later rates must come
from a separately cited current-law schedule, not extrapolation.

The data are used for historical **description only**. No claim of a causal
growth, investment, fiscal, or inequality effect can be established by
regressing national outcomes on this single rate series.
