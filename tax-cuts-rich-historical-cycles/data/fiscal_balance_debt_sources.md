# Federal fiscal balance and debt held by the public

Primary sources: U.S. Office of Management and Budget (OMB), distributed by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis via [FRED FYFSGDA188S](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/FYFSGDA188S) (*Federal Surplus or Deficit [-] as Percent of Gross Domestic Product*) and [FRED FYPUGDA188S](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/FYPUGDA188S) (*Gross Federal Debt Held by the Public as Percent of Gross Domestic Product*). Both tables retrieved 2026-11-02, latest stated update 2026-10-28. The annual 1964–2018 observations are frozen as reported at five decimal places; future OMB revisions may alter the live series.

FRED computes the fiscal balance ratio as `((FYFSD / 1000) / GDPA) * 100`, using OMB federal fiscal-year surplus/deficit and annual nominal GDP. Negative values denote deficits. Debt ratio uses `(FYGFDPUB / GDPA) * 100`. The debt stock is **debt held by the public**, not gross federal debt including intragovernmental holdings; the numerator is a fiscal stock and the annual GDP denominator a flow. The year labels inherit OMB/FRED fiscal conventions, not perfectly matched calendar periods. The source's formal debt series title contains “Gross Federal Debt Held by the Public”; this is not the gross federal debt total.

Deficits reflect revenues **and** expenditures, automatic stabilizers, interest, wars, recessions, demographics, and policy timing. Debt stock changes need not equal the single-year deficit due to valuation, cash balances, and GDP growth. These plotted correlations do **not** attribute the observed deficit or debt trajectory to tax cuts. Do not infer fiscal self-financing from gross tax-rate or receipt movements without a model-based counterfactual. The other source `federal_receipts_gdp_1964_2018.csv` concerns total receipts, not high-income taxpayer liabilities.
