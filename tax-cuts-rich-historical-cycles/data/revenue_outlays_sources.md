# Federal receipts and net outlays, 1964–2018

Sources: U.S. Office of Management and Budget (OMB), via FRED [FYONGDA188S](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/FYONGDA188S), *Federal Net Outlays as Percent of Gross Domestic Product*, and the already frozen [FYFRGDA188S](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/FYFRGDA188S), *Federal Receipts as Percent of Gross Domestic Product*. Both FRED ratios divide OMB fiscal-year nominal amounts by BEA annual nominal GDP and multiply by 100. Data viewed 2026-11-02; rounded here exactly as displayed to five decimals. These are retrospective and subject to revision.

The identity `federal receipts - federal net outlays = federal surplus (or deficit)` is checked against the independent frozen FRED `FYFSGDA188S` balance series. The largest observed residual across 55 separately published observations is 0.00013 percentage points (1966); the validation tolerance is 0.00015 percentage points. That discrepancy is negligible for the figure but exceeds naive five-decimal rounding error, so the source values are retained unadjusted. This **accounting identity is not a causal decomposition**. It cannot assign any part of a change in revenue or spending to a tax statute, nor can it distinguish automatic stabilizers from discretionary actions. The ratios also change with GDP in the denominator.

Outlays here are **net federal outlays**, not total spending by all levels of government, and receipts include all federal taxes and other receipts, not just income taxes paid by the highest earners. The identity does not decompose outlays by function or receipts by source; that requires different OMB tables. Do not use before/after differences as tax-act counterfactuals.
