# CBO household resources in 2018 — provenance and limits

Primary source: Congressional Budget Office, *The Distribution of Household Income, 2018* (August 2021), https://www.cbo.gov/publication/57404, Summary and Exhibit 1, with the middle-quintile after-tax figure in the report's distribution discussion. The report's figures are CBO estimates, not raw administrative records. Amounts are rounded to the nearest $100 and measured in **2018 dollars**.

The three rows are **selected** quintiles, not the complete distribution. Households are ranked by income before transfers and taxes, adjusted for household size; each quintile contains about one-fifth of the **population**, not necessarily one-fifth of households. The lowest group excludes households with negative income. Before-transfer-and-tax income includes market income **and social insurance** (for example Social Security and Medicare); after-transfer-and-tax income adds means-tested transfers and subtracts federal individual income, payroll, corporate, and excise taxes under CBO's incidence assumptions. The corporate burden is assigned to households through the CBO model, not determined by the identity of the statutory payer.

Source transcription, selected group averages: lowest $22,500 before / $37,700 after; middle $77,500 before / $71,100 after; highest $321,700 before / $243,900 after. These are group averages of different concepts **within the same year**. Their differences are **not** effects of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. No standard errors are provided for this extracted illustrative subset.

The same CBO report separately models the 2017 tax act against a prior-law counterfactual. It estimates lower average federal rates across all five quintiles, with individual-income-tax provisions reducing average rates by roughly 1.0–1.3 percentage points across quintiles and corporate/pass-through provisions providing the greatest reductions to the highest quintile. Those policy-attribution findings rely on a model and **must not** be inferred from the before/after-income bars here. Average federal effective rates also must not be compared directly to the top statutory marginal-rate series.
