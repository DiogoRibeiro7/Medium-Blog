# OMB federal outlays by superfunction, 1964–2018

Source: U.S. Office of Management and Budget (OMB), *Historical Tables, Fiscal Year 2027 Budget*, Table 3.1, “Outlays by Superfunction and Function: 1940–2031,” spreadsheet https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/hist03z1_fy2027.xlsx, linked from https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/budget/historical-tables/ (accessed 2026-11-02).

Only the **As percentages of GDP** section is transcribed for fiscal years 1964–2018. The six mutually exclusive superfunctions are national defense, human resources, physical resources, net interest, other functions, and **undistributed offsetting receipts**, which is negative in many years. The separate total federal outlays is also preserved. These values are percentages of nominal GDP rounded to one decimal, and do not represent rates of taxation or household incidence. The six components can deviate from the rounded total by up to about 0.2 percentage points in this extracted window; the renderer validates at 0.301 to allow rounding.

“Human resources” is OMB's historical budget classification, grouping health, Medicare, Social Security, income security, education and related services. It is not identical to cash transfers, and its historical growth reflects demographics, inflation-adjusted program rules, health costs, and accounting changes. “Physical resources” includes transport, environment, energy, regional development, and some commerce/housing programs; it does not equal public capital investment or infrastructure expenditures. “Other functions” is not the same as the OMB receipts category “other.” Net interest is a distinct fiscal cost. Offsetting receipts are displayed as a negative aggregate, not silently discarded.

For consistency, do not force this FY2027 OMB vintage to match the older FRED net-outlays/GDP series already frozen in the project. Differences across vintages reflect revised historical figures and denominators. These data describe composition; they do not identify the causal effects of the Kennedy, Reagan, Bush or 2017 tax reforms.
