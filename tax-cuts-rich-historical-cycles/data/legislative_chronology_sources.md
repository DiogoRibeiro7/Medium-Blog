# Legislative chronology and statutory-claim audit

Reviewed 2026-11-02. Sources are enacted public laws or Congressional records; the IRS/FRED annual rate series is a separate descriptive dataset. **Dates of enactment, implementation and observed effects must not be conflated.**

| Episode | Enactment / authority | Statutory mechanism relevant to the essay | Audit boundary |

| Revenue Act of 1964 | P.L. 88-272, enacted 26 February 1964; [authenticated statute](https://www.congress.gov/88/statute/STATUTE-78/STATUTE-78-Pg19.pdf) | Individual and corporate tax reductions, with individual reductions phased over tax years 1964 and 1965. Section 1 explicitly asserted that stimulation would eventually *raise*, rather than lower, revenues. | Congress's stated expectation is **not evidence that the forecast was realized**. Do not call the 1964 statutory 77% bracket a 1963 rate or an effective rate. |
| Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 | P.L. 97-34, enacted 13 August 1981; [authenticated statute](https://www.congress.gov/97/statute/STATUTE-95/STATUTE-95-Pg172.pdf) | Individual rate cuts phased in, with the maximum rate on non-earned income reduced from 70% to 50% effective for 1982. Prior law already capped earned-income marginal rates at 50%. | 1981 enactment is not equivalent to complete 1981 implementation; the 1981 annual 69.125% series observation should not be relabelled as 50%. |
| Tax Reform Act of 1986 | P.L. 99-514, enacted 22 October 1986; [contemporaneous Congressional Record account](https://www.congress.gov/100/crecb/1987/05/04/GPO-CRECB-1987-pt8-6.pdf) | Lower statutory individual rates alongside broader bases, eliminated shelters, changes to capital-formation provisions and a stronger minimum tax; main 15%/28% individual schedule fully operative in 1988. | Transition-year 1987 (38.5% in the frozen series) must remain separate from 1988 (28%). The 28% headline was not the maximum marginal burden for every high-income taxpayer because of phase-outs/surcharge effects. |
| Economic Growth and Tax Relief Reconciliation Act of 2001 | P.L. 107-16, enacted 7 June 2001; [authenticated statute](https://www.congress.gov/107/plaws/publ16/PLAW-107publ16.pdf) | New 10% bracket and scheduled reductions in individual marginal rates, with timing/phase-in and sunset provisions. | Do not attribute the entirety of the 2001–03 receipts change to this law or treat the 2001 cut as immediately fully phased in. |
| Jobs and Growth Tax Relief Reconciliation Act of 2003 | P.L. 108-27, enacted 28 May 2003; [authenticated statute](https://www.congress.gov/108/plaws/publ27/PLAW-108publ27.pdf) | Accelerated selected 2001 individual-rate changes; modified dividends and capital-gains taxation and investment incentives. | Not simply a duplicate of the 2001 act; short-run stimulation and long-run supply-side claims require different counterfactuals. |

## Primary-source interpretation

The original text of the 1964 law is unusually valuable as evidence of the *political claim being made*: Section 1 predicted that stimulus from the reduction would, after a transition, yield higher revenue. A statement in a statute establishes an argument made by lawmakers. It is neither a forecast error calculation nor an estimate of causal tax feedback. Evaluating it requires an independent no-reform fiscal counterfactual.

The 1981 and 1986 laws also underscore that a single annual top statutory bracket cannot describe tax-base changes, the earned-income cap, phase-ins or minimum-tax effects. The companion's annual series is descriptive; it should not be used as a comprehensive effective-tax-rate panel.
