Part 1: the premises of 'taxation is theft', and the one that data can test.
NumPy 2.5.3

==============================================================================================
1. The economy
==============================================================================================
Endowment E = 20 tokens per person and round; groups of n = 4; MPCR = 0.4.
Marginal group return G = n x MPCR = 1.6: a token given creates 1.6 tokens of value,
of which the giver gets back 0.4. Keeping the token is privately better whatever the others do;
giving everything is collectively best.
Welfare per person and round, in units of E: none given = 1.00; everything given = 1.60.
Voluntary provision share s gives 1 + (G - 1) s; a tax raising the whole endowment, with marginal
cost of public funds lam and a share w of revenue lost before it reaches the good, gives
1 - lam + G (1 - w). Compulsion is better exactly when s < s* = (G (1 - w) - lam) / (G - 1).

==============================================================================================
2. Voluntary contributions: calibration and path
==============================================================================================
Model: person i gives beta_i x (belief about the others' average) + error, clipped to [0, E];
beliefs move towards what the others gave: b <- theta b + (1 - theta) observed.
beta_i ~ Beta with mean beta_mean and concentration 4; first-round beliefs ~ N(belief0, 0.2);
decision error sd 0.1 of E; theta = 0.3. Calibrated: beta_mean and belief0.
Targets: round 1 = 0.50 (Ledyard 1995: 40-60 %); round 10 = 0.20 (Andreoni 1995 / Isaac and Walker 1988: 15-25 %).
Calibrated: beta_mean = 0.829, belief0 = 0.602 (squared error below 1e-8).

==============================================================================================
3. Peer punishment
==============================================================================================
After contributions are seen, people with slope beta_i >= 0.5 punish each member in proportion
to that member's shortfall below the rest of the group. A point costs the punisher 1 token and the
target 3. Points received raise the target's contribution floor; floors do not relax (decay 1.0).
Targets: in round 10, 82.5% give the whole endowment (Fehr and Gaechter 2000, final
period of the partner treatment with punishment); welfare over 10 rounds equal to the voluntary
regime's (Fehr and Williams 2017, on Fehr and Gaechter 2000: the total cost of punishment was on
average as large as the benefit from the extra cooperation).
Calibrated: gamma = 11.65 points per unit of shortfall, eta = 0.0772 (squared error below 1e-8).
Mean contribution share by round (1-10): [0.5   0.537 0.616 0.722 0.808 0.87  0.911 0.937 0.953 0.964]
Share giving the whole endowment by round (1-10): [0.015 0.07  0.102 0.2   0.346 0.494 0.619 0.71  0.778 0.825]
Punishment cost per person by round (1-10), share of E: [0.673 0.569 0.537 0.383 0.248 0.156 0.096 0.06  0.038 0.025]
Rounds 20, 30, 40, 50: contribution [0.99  0.995 0.996 0.997] cost [0.002 0.001 0.    0.   ]
Welfare, sum over rounds: 10 rounds 11.907 (ratio to voluntary 1.000); 50 rounds 75.634 (ratio 1.381).
Cumulative welfare gap (punishment minus voluntary): lowest after round 4 (-1.744 E per person); positive for good from round 11.
Per-round welfare with punishment, rounds 1-10: [0.627 0.753 0.833 1.05  1.237 1.366 1.451 1.502 1.534 1.553]
Out-of-sample check: Gaechter, Renner and Sefton (2008) found that over 50 rounds punishment
raised both cooperation and earnings. The model calibrated on 10 rounds gives a 50-round welfare
ratio of 1.381. (Their 50-round groups also show an end-game drop, which this model lacks.)
Voluntary share with the same welfare: 10 rounds 0.318; 50 rounds 0.854.

With floors that relax by 10 % a round (decay 0.9), the two targets cannot both be met:
  best fit gamma 13.00, eta 1.000: full givers in round 10 0.548, 10-round welfare ratio 0.978 (squared error 7.7e-02).

Antisocial punishment (Herrmann, Thoeni and Gaechter 2008): a share of punishers also punishes
those who gave more than they did; antisocial points lower the target's slope and floor.
  antisocial share 0.0: welfare ratio 10 rounds 1.000, 50 rounds 1.381; contribution share round 10 0.964, round 50 0.997
  antisocial share 0.1: welfare ratio 10 rounds 0.918, 50 rounds 1.346; contribution share round 10 0.944, round 50 0.992
  antisocial share 0.2: welfare ratio 10 rounds 0.793, 50 rounds 1.247; contribution share round 10 0.917, round 50 0.978
  antisocial share 0.3: welfare ratio 10 rounds 0.616, 50 rounds 1.067; contribution share round 10 0.883, round 50 0.954
  antisocial share 0.5: welfare ratio 10 rounds 0.148, 50 rounds 0.423; contribution share round 10 0.791, round 50 0.865
