7. What the literature's elasticities imply for the number of new medicines
==============================================================================================
Baseline: 104 new molecules first launched worldwide in 2025 (EFPIA). Elasticity of the number of new medicines to the size of their potential market: 0.25 (Dubois, de Mouzon, Scott Morton and Seabright 2015) and 4 (Acemoglu and Linn 2004, new non-generic drugs). Changes in market size are taken as shares of the world market.
  Portugal's share of the market for new medicines is taken to be its share of the whole market (an upper bound, since new medicines reach Portugal later); the five largest European markets' share is EFPIA's 15.6%.
  Portugal spends 25% more on all medicines: market for new medicines +0.08%; new medicines +0.02% (0.02 a year) to +0.3% (0.3 a year)
  Portugal doubles what it spends on all medicines: market for new medicines +0.34%; new medicines +0.08% (0.09 a year) to +1.3% (1.4 a year)
  the five largest European markets pay 25% more for new medicines: market for new medicines +3.90%; new medicines +0.97% (1.01 a year) to +15.6% (16.2 a year)
  the five largest European markets pay US net prices for new medicines (x 3.08): market for new medicines +32.45%; new medicines +8.11% (8.44 a year) to +129.8% (135.0 a year)
  one more new medicine a year needs a world market larger by 3.8% at elasticity 0.25 and 0.24% at elasticity 4; Portugal's whole market is 0.34% of the world's
Part 2: US against French prices, single-source molecules (n=113), Fisher index 5.820 before rebates
