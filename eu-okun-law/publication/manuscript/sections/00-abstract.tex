\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We re-examine the relation between quarterly real GDP growth and changes in the
unemployment rate in the \val{n_countries} member states of the European Union from 2009
to 2026. The data are official Eurostat series frozen with cryptographic hashes. Each
stage of the analysis was registered before its own estimation, sequentially, on the same
data.

\textbf{Association.} With 2020--2021 excluded, the two-lag Okun coefficient sum is
negative in \val{hi_point_negative} of \val{n_countries} countries. Under small-sample
bootstraps that hold their size, it is significantly negative in \val{hi_negative}. How
many of these are established depends on the error rate controlled across countries:
\val{nr_bh_reg} if it is the false discovery rate (Benjamini--Hochberg, applied after the
registered run and assuming positive dependence), \val{hi_negative_holm} under the
registered Holm adjustment, which controls the chance of any false rejection. Normal
critical values, which over-reject here, give \val{cl_n_neg_excluded} unadjusted. The
sign is widespread. Including the pandemic
quarters pulls most sums towards zero. A hierarchical Bayesian model with Student-$t$
errors, residual autocorrelation and a common European factor estimates the association
beyond the common European cycle. Across accepted specifications, the unweighted mean of
the country sums lies between \val{by_mu_lo} and \val{by_mu_hi}. The primary model
gives \val{by_mu} \val{by_mu_ci}, but it understates the persistence of unemployment
changes. The amended model that passes that check gives \val{dyn_mu} \val{dyn_mu_ci} for
the long-run sum. Whether \val{by_n_neg_all} or \val{by_n_neg_unpooled} countries are
credibly negative in every accepted fit depends on one fit at the convergence threshold.
Country ranks are not established.

\textbf{Prediction.} Over all \val{fc_origins} target quarters, none of the registered
one-quarter-ahead forecast comparisons is decided; the pandemic targets dominate the
loss differences. Without the pandemic targets, a registered descriptive analysis finds
gains of \val{fcx_gain_lo} to \val{fcx_gain_hi} from partial pooling, lagged GDP growth
and the common factor. Even there, no model reliably beats a forecast that the
unemployment rate will not change.

\textbf{Causation.} External instruments for monetary-policy and oil-supply shocks are weak
for real activity: first-stage $|t|$ is at most \val{id_max_t} quarterly and
\val{mo_max_t} monthly. A foreign-demand shift-share instrument is infeasible (expected
first-stage $t$ of \val{ss_P1_t}). The sign restrictions that the data admit leave the
shock-conditional Okun ratios unbounded. These instruments and restrictions are
uninformative; they are not evidence about the size of any causal effect.

\textbf{Countries.} The study was prompted by an earlier analysis that described a large
association for Spain, a delayed one for the Netherlands and a weak one for Luxembourg,
and suggested measurement and institutional explanations. We test two of that kind: a
commuter mismatch in Luxembourg and employment protection. The Spanish
claim is supported in every accepted specification. The Dutch and Luxembourg claims are
qualified. In Luxembourg, output per hour moves almost one for one with measured output.
Cross-border commuters and the large financial sector fail their registered tests, and
transitory noise in quarterly output is not established. Luxembourg's weak link remains
unexplained.

Every Okun coefficient in this study is a descriptive association; none is an identified
causal effect.
\end{abstract}
