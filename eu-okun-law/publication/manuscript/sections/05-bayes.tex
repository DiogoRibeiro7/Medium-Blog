\section{Hierarchical Bayesian estimates}\label{sec:bayes}

\subsection{Model}

The primary model keeps $\theta_i$ as the coefficient sum but lets countries borrow
strength from one another, and it models the dependence that the classical regressions
leave in the residuals. Write $y_{it}=\Delta u_{it}$ and reparameterise the lag weights so
that $\theta_i$ appears directly:
\begin{align}
  y_{it} &= \alpha_i + \theta_i\, g_{it} + \sum_{k=1}^{K} b_{ik}\,(g_{i,t-k}-g_{it})
            + \lambda_i f_t + e_{it}, \label{eq:bayes}\\
  e_{it} &= \phi_i\, e_{i,t-1} + \sigma_i\,\eta_{it},\qquad \eta_{it}\sim t_\nu ,
\end{align}
with $K=2$. Here:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $f_t\sim N(0,1)$ is a common quarterly factor with non-negative loadings
    $\lambda_i$, representing European shocks not captured by each country's own growth;
  \item the AR(1) term captures residual persistence;
  \item Student-$t$ innovations allow for outlying quarters.
\end{itemize}
Country parameters are partially pooled \citep{gelman2013bda} through non-centred
hierarchies, for example
$\theta_i=\mu_\theta+\tau_\theta z_i$ with $z_i\sim N(0,1)$. Analogous hierarchies apply
to $\alpha_i$, $b_{ik}$, $\phi_i$ (on the $\tanh$ scale) and $\log\sigma_i$.

\paragraph{Priors.} The prior on the mean sum, $\mu_\theta\sim N(0,0.5^2)$, is centred at
zero, so the prior does not assume Okun's law. The other priors are
$\tau_\theta\sim N^+(0,0.25^2)$, $\lambda_i\sim N^+(0,0.2^2)$ and
$\nu\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(2,0.1)$. Prior predictive checks show that these weakly informative
priors put visible mass on implausibly large quarterly changes: \val{bd_prior_share} of
simulated changes exceed 5 pp, and the median largest simulated change is
\val{bd_prior_largest} pp, against \val{bd_obs_largest} pp in the data. Most of that mass
comes from the GDP coefficients acting on large GDP swings: with those terms set to zero,
the median largest change is \val{bd_prior_largest_nogdp} pp. The priors are reported
rather than tuned; a calibrated alternative is a diagnostic below.

\paragraph{Estimands.} $\mu_\theta$ is the mean of the population distribution of country
sums: an unweighted cross-country average. It is not an EU aggregate and not a pooled
slope. With the common factor in the model, $\theta_i$ measures the association with each
country's GDP growth beyond the common European cycle.

\paragraph{Sample.} The primary sample is 2009-Q2 to 2019-Q4: the longest window that is
contiguous and definition-consistent in every country.

\subsection{Posterior inference}

\paragraph{Sampler.} Posteriors are sampled with the No-U-Turn sampler
\citep{hoffman2014nuts} in NumPyro \citep{phan2019numpyro}: four chains of 1000 warm-up and
1000 retained draws, target acceptance $0.95$, fixed seeds.

\paragraph{Acceptance.} A fit is accepted only if all four conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the rank-normalised $\widehat R\le 1.01$ \citep{vehtari2021rhat};
  \item bulk and tail effective sample sizes are at least 400;
  \item there are no divergences;
  \item E-BFMI $\ge 0.3$.
\end{itemize}
A failed fit is reported and not interpreted.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.} The sensitivity analysis fixed \val{by_n_specs} specifications in
advance: wide priors, strong
pooling, no pooling, no common factor, Gaussian i.i.d.\ errors, the participation control,
four lags, and a sample without 2020--2021. Of these, \val{by_n_accepted} of
\val{by_n_specs} were accepted; the unpooled fit missed the $\widehat R$ threshold.

\paragraph{Model comparison and checks.} Models are compared by PSIS-LOO
\citep{vehtari2017loo} and checked with posterior predictive statistics.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{Average and spread.} In the primary fit the mean country sum is
$\mu_\theta=$ \val{by_mu} (90\% credible interval \val{by_mu_ci}), and the
between-country standard deviation is $\tau_\theta=$ \val{by_tau} (\val{by_tau_ci}).
The primary model fails one predictive check (below), so this is one point in a range
rather than the answer: across accepted specifications the posterior median of
$\mu_\theta$ runs from \val{by_mu_lo} (Gaussian i.i.d.\ errors) to \val{by_mu_hi}. The
amended model that passes the persistence check and is preferred to the primary model by
PSIS-LOO gives
\val{dyn_mu} \val{dyn_mu_ci} for the mean long-run sum. In every factor specification the
estimand is the association beyond the common European cycle: the fitted factor
correlates \val{by_factor_corr} with mean GDP growth.

\paragraph{Country signs.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \val{by_n_neg_primary} of \val{n_countries} country sums are credibly negative,
    meaning $P(\theta_i<0)\ge 0.95$, in the primary fit.
  \item \val{by_n_neg_all} are credibly negative in every accepted specification. This
    count depends on one fit. The unpooled specification narrowly missed the
    $\widehat R$ threshold (\val{by_unpooled_rhat}, on \val{by_unpooled_country}'s AR(1)
    coefficient), and the Linux replication accepted it. With it, the count is
    \val{by_n_neg_unpooled}.
  \item The \val{by_unpooled_n_differ} countries in between (\val{by_unpooled_differ})
    reach $P(\theta_i<0)$ of only \val{by_unpooled_p_lo} to \val{by_unpooled_p_hi} in the
    unpooled fit. OLS on the same quarters excludes zero for \val{by_unpooled_ols_n} of
    them (\val{by_unpooled_ols}), but not for \val{by_unpooled_not_ols}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Magnitudes.} The Bayesian sums are smaller in magnitude than OLS on the same
quarters in \val{by_n_smaller} of \val{n_countries} countries (table~\ref{tab:countries},
figure~\ref{fig:countries}). Pooling
explains only part of the difference. The error model and the common factor change the
estimand: the classical sum includes the common cycle, while the factor model's sum does
not. A classical estimator that also removes the common cycle, the CCE mean group of
section~\ref{sec:classical}, gives \val{cce_mean}, against \val{cce_mg_ols} for the plain
mean group.

\paragraph{Checks.} The posterior predictive checks find one systematic misfit:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the lag-1 autocorrelation of unemployment changes is \val{by_lag1_obs} in the data
    but \val{by_lag1_rep} on average in replicated data (posterior predictive $p=$
    \val{by_lag1_p}), so the primary model understates persistence;
  \item central 90\% one-step predictive intervals cover \val{by_cov90} of observations.
\end{itemize}
The persistence is not concentrated in the countries whose monthly rates are smoothed,
and their quarterly rates do not inherit that smoothing (section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
Without those six countries, the observed mean autocorrelation rises from
\val{sm_misfit_all} to \val{sm_misfit_without}.

\paragraph{Amendments.} A registered amendment added an AR(1) common factor and lagged
unemployment changes. Their combination removes the persistence misfit (posterior
predictive $p=$ \val{dyn_p}) and is preferred to the primary model by PSIS-LOO. That it
also improves on the lagged changes alone is not established (below). Its factor remains
correlated with average GDP growth (\val{dyn_factor_corr}). A second amendment allowed the
coefficient sums to shift after 2021: the average shift is \val{reg_delta}
(\val{reg_delta_ci}), with no credible change in any evaluable country. The country shifts
are pooled strongly ($\tau_\delta\approx$ \val{reg_tau}), so this mostly reflects low power
to detect country-specific shifts.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} A further protocol, registered after these results, checked them
in four ways. Its results sit alongside the registered ones and replace none of them.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Repeated runs.} Every fit in a registered model comparison was repeated with
    four further seeds, giving \val{bd_runs} runs with the local and Linux ones. The
    medians of the two mean sums above move by at most \val{bd_run_shift} between runs,
    and the credibly negative counts do not change. The amended model passes the
    persistence check in every run ($p$ from \val{bd_dyn_p_lo} to \val{bd_dyn_p_hi}).
  \item \emph{PSIS-LOO reliability.} The \val{bd_exact_cells} cells with Pareto
    $\hat k>0.7$ in any run were scored by exact leave-one-out refits.
    \val{bd_loo_est} of the \val{bd_loo_total} registered comparisons keep their verdict
    in every run and under exact leave-one-out, including the advantage of both factor
    models over the primary model. Whether the amended model improves on the lagged
    changes alone is not established: the elpd difference is \val{bd_adf_ardl_lo} to
    \val{bd_adf_ardl_hi} standard errors across runs, against a threshold of two.
  \item \emph{Influential cells.} Leaving out those cells, one at a time or all at once,
    moves neither mean sum by more than \val{bd_inf_shift}. It does make
    \val{bd_inf_added} credibly negative in some refits, and removes no country. These
    classifications sit at the 0.95 threshold.
  \item \emph{Prior calibration.} Halving every prior scale is the widest step of a fixed
    ladder at which at most 1\% of simulated changes exceed 5 pp. It gives
    $\mu_\theta=$ \val{bd_cal_mu} \val{bd_cal_mu_ci}, against \val{by_mu}, and a mean
    long-run sum of \val{bd_cal_dyn}, against \val{bd_dyn_reg}. It adds one country to
    each model's credibly negative set (\val{bd_cal_added}) and removes none. It also
    narrows the prior of the factor's AR(1) coefficient, whose median falls from
    \val{bd_rho} to \val{bd_cal_rho}; the amended model then no longer passes the
    persistence check ($p=$ \val{bd_cal_dyn_p}).
\end{itemize}
