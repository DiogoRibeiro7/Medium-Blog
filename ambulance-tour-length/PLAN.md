# Pre-registration: tour length and the hours that size an ambulance roster

Written on 21 November 2026, before any EMS record was downloaded or read. The NYC 311 hourly counts reused here
were analysed in the companion `staffing-shift-constraints` (#194) for 8-hour shifts only; nothing about them under
the other tour lengths below has been computed. Anything changed after this file is committed is listed under
"Changes after registration", with its reason and date, and marked as such in the article. The code was checked only
on synthetic data (`tests/`) before the data arrived.

## Question

A roster covers an hourly requirement with tours of a fixed length. The companion of #194 found that, with 8-hour
shifts at any start, the hours that set the size of a 311 roster were 01:00, 09:00 and 17:00, eight hours apart and
not at the peak, and conjectured that with tours of length L the binding hours lie L hours apart, anchored on the
steepest change in the daily requirement. This plan tests that conjecture on FDNY ambulance workload and on the same
311 calls, with tour lengths of 6, 8, 9, 10 and 12 hours.

The thesis being tested is that **the tour length sets the roster**: it decides which hours size the roster, where
in the day they fall, and how much idle time a roster built with perfect knowledge of the workload must carry.

## Mechanism stated in advance

With tours of one length L that may start at any hour of a cyclic week of 168 hours, the cheapest roster solves

minimise L x (number of tours), such that every hour h has at least need(h) tours on duty.

Its linear-programming dual is: choose weights y(h) >= 0 so that every window of L consecutive hours carries at most
L, and maximise the sum of need(h) y(h). Placing y = L on a set of hours that are at least L apart is feasible for
the dual, so the paid hours of any roster are at least L times the largest total requirement that can be collected
at hours at least L apart. Call such a set of hours a **comb** and the largest total the **comb value** V(L); L x V(L)
is the **comb bound**.

The bound need not be exact: the dual can do better with weights that are not of comb form. On 80 synthetic weekly
requirements (`tests/`, and the calibration noted under "Hypotheses"), the integer optimum equalled the linear
relaxation in every case, the comb bound equalled the optimum in 72 of 80 cells and was never more than 0.2% below
it; 86% of the roster's binding hours (defined below) belonged to an optimal comb, and 97% of the hours of optimal
combs were binding.

If the bound is close on real data, the binding hours are mostly the teeth of the best comb, and for an L that
divides 24, where the teeth sit in the day is the phase phi that maximises the sum of the mean daily requirement at
phi, phi + L, phi + 2L, ...: in Fourier terms, the phase of the daily profile's harmonics at period L and its
divisors. That is a different prediction from "anchored on the steepest change" and from "anchored on the peak", and
the test below compares the three.

## Data

**EMS.** NYC Open Data, *EMS Incident Dispatch Data* (`76xm-jjuj`), generated by FDNY's EMS computer-aided dispatch
system. `fetch.py` first records the dataset's column list and update time (`sources/schema.json`) and stops if any
of these fields is missing: `cad_incident_id`, `incident_datetime`, `first_assignment_datetime`,
`incident_close_datetime`, `borough`. It then downloads those fields for every incident first assigned from 30
December 2018 00:00 to 30 December 2019 00:00 (local clock time, as recorded), and writes only hourly aggregates:
`sources/ems_hourly.csv`, one row per local clock hour and borough, with the busy unit-minutes and the number of
incidents assigned in that hour. Its SHA-256 goes in `sources/FILES`, the download counts in `sources/REVISION`.

**Busy time.** An incident keeps one ambulance busy from its first assignment to its close. Incidents without both
times, with a close at or before the assignment, or lasting more than 8 hours are excluded and counted. The data
record one assignment time per incident, so incidents served by more than one unit are counted once; the workload
is a lower bound.

**311.** The hourly counts of agent-handled calls to NYC 311 committed with #194 (`sources/nyc311_hourly_calls.csv`,
SHA-256 `b89dfb31ec9b6cf3f8741cb7bb86d822370e0354338a50b5149f8e1b9c66d2de`).

**Datasets.** Seven requirement series: EMS citywide, EMS in each of the five boroughs (each borough a separate pool
of ambulances), and 311.

## Windows

The 52 weeks from Monday 31 December 2018 to Sunday 29 December 2019, the test weeks of #194. Weeks run from Monday
00:00 to Sunday 23:59, local clock time. Each week is rostered on its own as a cyclic week: a tour that runs past
Sunday midnight covers the first hours of the same week. The roster uses the workload that actually occurred (a
perfect forecast); forecasting is not part of this test.

## Requirement per hour

- **EMS:** the offered load of hour h is a(h) = busy unit-minutes in h / 60, the mean number of ambulances busy. The
  requirement is the smallest number of ambulances s > a(h) for which an M/M/s queue (Erlang C) gives a probability
  of at most 10% that an incident finds no ambulance free. An hour with no load needs none.
- **311:** as in #194: the smallest number of agents answering at least 80% of calls within 30 seconds (Erlang C),
  with a 5-minute average handle time.

## Rosters

For each dataset, week and tour length L in {6, 8, 9, 10, 12}: tours of length L starting at any hour of the cyclic
week, minimising paid hours subject to covering every hour's requirement; integer programme solved with HiGHS
through `scipy.optimize.milp`, and its linear relaxation with `linprog`. Seven datasets x 52 weeks x 5 lengths =
1,820 cells.

## Measures

- **Binding hours** of a cell: the hours h for which adding one to need(h) raises the optimum of the integer
  programme (one re-solve per hour): the hours whose coverage sets the size of the roster.
- **Comb value** V(L): the largest sum of need(h) over a set of hours of the cyclic week pairwise at least L apart,
  by dynamic programming; **comb hours**: the hours that belong to at least one optimal comb.
- **Binding share by clock hour:** for each dataset and L, f(k) = the share of the 364 test days on which clock hour
  k is a binding hour.
- **Mean daily profile** m(k): the mean requirement at clock hour k over the 364 test days.
- **Three predicted phases**, for L in {6, 8, 12} (the lengths that divide 24): the **comb rule** phi = argmax over
  phi in 0..L-1 of the sum of m(phi + jL), j = 0..24/L - 1; the **steepest-climb rule** (the #194 conjecture):
  phi = k* mod L, with k* the clock hour with the largest increase m(k) - m(k-1); the **peak rule**: phi = (argmax
  m) mod L.
- **Observed phase:** argmax over phi in 0..L-1 of the sum of f(phi + jL).
- **Idle share:** (paid hours - sum of need) / sum of need, over the 52 weeks.

## Hypotheses

- **H1, the comb sizes the roster.** Over the 1,092 cells with L in {6, 8, 12}: the comb bound equals the integer
  optimum in at least 80% of cells and is never more than 1% below it; at least 75% of all binding hours are comb
  hours; and at least 90% of all comb hours are binding hours. (Thresholds set from the synthetic calibration above.
  A second synthetic run, a pure daily sine wave with noise, left the bound loose for L = 9 and 10, where 168 is not
  a multiple of L and the dual spreads its weight; the same measures are reported for 9 and 10 without a threshold.)
- **H2, the comb rule anchors the chain.** For L in {6, 8, 12}, the comb rule's phase equals the observed phase in at
  least 19 of the 21 (dataset, L) cases, and in more cases than the steepest-climb rule and than the peak rule.
- **H3, tours that do not divide the day have no fixed binding hours.** The largest f(k) is above 0.5 for L in
  {6, 8, 12} and below 0.5 for L in {9, 10}, in at least 6 of the 7 datasets for each L.

## Also reported

- The idle share by dataset and L, and the paid hours relative to 8-hour tours.
- The number of binding hours per week against the most a comb can hold, floor(168 / L).
- The amplitude and phase of the mean daily profile's harmonics, and the three rules' predicted phases.
- Binding hours on weekdays and at weekends.
- **Short tours:** for L = 8 and 12, EMS citywide and 311, with 4-hour tours added (at most 25% of paid hours), the
  share of 4-hour tours whose four hours include at least one binding hour of the L-only roster, against 4 / L, the
  share expected for a tour placed at random when the binding hours are L apart.
- **Sensitivity of the EMS requirement:** H1 to H3 with a 2% probability of finding no ambulance free, and with the
  requirement set to the offered load rounded up (no margin).
- Data checks: incidents kept and excluded, and hours with no load.
