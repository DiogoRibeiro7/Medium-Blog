4. In-control data needed for a distribution-free guarantee
==============================================================================
Threshold = the largest of n exchangeable in-control scores (one-sided). Its exceedance probability is
Beta(1, n), so it is <= alpha with probability 1 - eps once n >= log(eps) / log(1 - alpha) (Wilks 1941).
   one 3-sigma-equivalent chart, p0: alpha 0.0027; n for eps 0.10: 852, eps 0.05: 1109; with the second largest as threshold (eps 0.10): 1440
   a line budget of 1 alarm in 100 runs: alpha 0.01; n for eps 0.10: 230, eps 0.05: 299; with the second largest as threshold (eps 0.10): 388
   one of 428 sensors under a 1-in-100 line budget (Sidak): alpha 2.348e-05; n for eps 0.10: 98058, eps 0.05: 127576; with the second largest as threshold (eps 0.10): 165647
   a line budget of 1 alarm per hour, scored once a minute: alpha 0.01667; n for eps 0.10: 138, eps 0.05: 179; with the second largest as threshold (eps 0.10): 232
   one of 21 series under a 1-per-hour line budget, once a minute (Sidak): alpha 0.0008; n for eps 0.10: 2878, eps 0.05: 3744; with the second largest as threshold (eps 0.10): 4861

worked example: 50 charts, one point every 10 minutes, Phase I of 100 points per chart; budget one false
alarm per 8-hour shift for the line, i.e. a line false-alarm probability of 1/48 per sampling time
   per-chart Sidak rate 4.210e-04; widths: Sidak 3.527, every chart guaranteed 4.429, joint line guarantee 3.724
   Sidak: median ARL of one chart after a 2-sigma shift 15.4 points = 2.6 hours
   every chart: median ARL of one chart after a 2-sigma shift 126.4 points = 21.1 hours
   joint line: median ARL of one chart after a 2-sigma shift 23.0 points = 3.8 hours
   distribution-free: one line score needs 110 in-control points (2.3 shifts); each chart separately needs 5469 (38 days at 144 points a day)

a validation-quantile alert threshold: the 10th largest of 1000 in-control scores (the empirical 99th
percentile). Exceedance probability ~ Beta(10, 991): mean 0.00999; P(exceedance > 1%) = 0.457; P(exceedance > 1.5%) = 0.068
